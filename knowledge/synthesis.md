# Research synthesis: develop capability and enable its use

Status: editorial synthesis of [R1 and R2](README.md), with selective checks in [EVIDENCE.md](research/source-register.md). This is not a systematic review. Reference notation `R1 §3` means section 3 of the domain briefing; `R2 fn29` means footnote 29 of the skills report.

## What the two reports contribute

R1 provides the broader OE argument: work design, mandates, organisational learning, intervention choices and university examples. R2 contributes finer distinctions about evidence of development, assessment, adaptive expertise and Leader Me. They overlap substantially and share sources; agreement between them is not independent replication.

The most useful common proposition is that development should improve situated judgement while exposing the conditions that enable or prevent action. The strongest practical correction is to separate learning, assisted performance, sustained application and organisational outcomes.

## Working vocabulary, not a universal ontology

| Term | Working meaning | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Understanding concepts, facts and methods | Reliable application |
| Skill | Learned ability to perform an identifiable activity | Competence in every context |
| Competency | A context- or role-specific bundle of knowledge, skills and behaviours | A universal psychological quantity |
| Capability | Ability to achieve a relevant result under stated conditions | That organisational capability is the sum of individual scores |
| Expertise | Domain-specific judgement, including exceptions and limits | That tenure alone proves it |
| Adaptive expertise | Using established practice and revising it for unfamiliar problems | A generic adaptability score |
| Professional identity | A revisable account of one's role, commitments and belonging | A list of skills or a personality diagnosis |
| Routine | A recurring pattern of coordinated activity | That a documented process describes actual work |

Sources: R1 §§1–2; R2 §2. These are useful analytical conventions; disciplinary meanings differ. OE is better treated here as a field of practice with multiple capabilities than as one assessable skill.

## Theory as a choice of question

The following are report-derived conceptual lenses, not a unified validated intervention.

| Lens | Useful question | Limit / misuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Argyris and Schön: single-/double-loop learning | Are we improving execution within a rule that itself needs questioning? | Not every problem needs a debate about fundamental assumptions |
| Lave and Wenger: situated learning | Who gets access to real cases, experienced peers and legitimate participation? | A community can reproduce exclusion and poor practice |
| Experiential learning and transfer | What changed between attempt, feedback and the next application? | Experience or reflection volume alone does not prove learning |
| Absorptive capacity / dynamic capabilities | How does external knowledge become decisions and repeatable organisational action? | Avoid explaining success circularly as evidence of capability |
| Sociotechnical systems / work design | Do tasks, technology, autonomy and workload allow the desired behaviour? | More autonomy without support can become extra burden |
| Adaptive leadership | Which aspects have known solutions, and which require learning and negotiation? | Complexity does not remove leadership responsibility |
| Psychological safety / self-determination | What happens after dissent, and is development meaningful and influenceable? | Pleasant meetings do not establish safety or real agency |
| Systems and complexity perspectives | What feedback, delays and competing interests shape the result? | “Complex” must not replace precise diagnosis |

Reading anchors and primary-source leads: R1 §2 and footnotes 3, 5–7, 10–17; see [catalogue](research/reports/catalogue.json). Full primary-source verification remains open.

## Evidence: what we can use cautiously

| Theme | Current judgement | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training and transfer | The reports identify meta-analytic support for training and links with work conditions | Moderator associations do not establish that every design feature causes improvement; most underlying reviews not rechecked here |
| Communities of practice | Plausible mechanisms connect relevant cases, peer exchange and subsequent application | Evidence from health and public-sector settings does not validate this Zürich community; no outcomes yet recorded |
| Reflection and debriefing | Structured reflection linked to another attempt is a defensible practice hypothesis | Journaling frequency is not competence; numerical effects remain report-derived |
| AI-supported work | Checked abstracts distinguish assisted task performance from independent knowledge | Effects depend on tasks, participants and implementation; two different studies need not give the same learning result |
| Gamification and character | Potentially useful for motivation or remembering practice | Education findings do not validate an OE avatar, XP rules or workplace mastery |
| Skill inference | Treat inferred skills as contestable claims with provenance | A journal, self-report or AI-produced work sample cannot establish stable competence on its own |

Checked AI examples: Wiles et al. (2026) studied 487 consultants and found better assisted task performance without evidence of improved subsequent unaided technical answers. Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (2025) studied 5,172 support agents during staggered AI introduction; their abstract reports productivity gains and evidence of learning. Different tasks and designs prohibit a blanket “AI teaches” or “AI prevents learning” conclusion. [V4–V5](research/source-register.md)

## Strategic choices

| Approach | Useful when | Cost / failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared taxonomy | Teams need a language for a specific recurring decision | Maintenance and false equivalence across roles |
| Strategy-led capability programme | Leadership has chosen a concrete organisational outcome | Training can conceal unresolved strategic trade-offs |
| Local development priorities | Needs and practices differ meaningfully | Fragmentation and unequal access to learning |
| Professional communities | Judgement and tacit knowledge matter | Exchange without application; insider networks |
| Work redesign | Conditions prevent capable people from acting | Requires real authority over tasks, time and incentives |
| Skills-based staffing | Relevant expertise is hard to locate or mobilise | Matching without release capacity; biased visibility |

Editorial recommendation for a university: a modest common language, explicit decision rights, locally defined work problems and professional peer connections. Test that combination against real cases before investing in a large taxonomy or platform. It is a proposal, not a description of current University of Basel policy.

## Implications for Leader Me, kept separate from domain knowledge

Preserve practice history while allowing interpretations to change. Record missing opportunity separately from failed performance; preserve counterevidence and disagreement. Distinguish exposure, understanding, attempt, observed quality, transfer and outcome without assuming a causal ladder. Map any future OE capability content against an existing professional framework before adding categories.

The reports do not validate the complete Leader Me learning loop. The existing milestones remain product conventions. No research finding here settles XP, assessment choice, sharing architecture or AI coaching implementation.
