# Skills in the Wild

*Fähigkeiten entwickeln. Spielräume schaffen. Im Alltag erproben.*

Status: concept, 12 November 2026. The owner endorsed the umbrella, wilderness theme, campaign orientation and domain-specific application scenarios. Delivery design and technical choices below are proposals. No campaign has been tested, app built or deployment authorised by this document.

## Purpose

Introduce skills as an approachable organisational topic and help people apply what they learn in real work. A campaign creates invitations and shared attention; a repeatable practice method turns that attention into attempts, feedback, improved judgement and useful routines.

This can be a month such as **Skillcember**, a longer season or a series of team expeditions. It is not limited to a short upskilling course. Joining one useful activity should be possible without completing a programme.

Central invitation: **Was in uns steckt, zeigt sich im Tun. Entdecke Fähigkeiten. Probiere Neues. Schaffe anderen den Spielraum dazu.**

## Campaign rhythm

1. **Explore / Gelände erkunden:** discover existing skills and useful expertise.
2. **Spot / Spuren lesen:** connect changes in work to future capability needs.
3. **Try / Neues Terrain betreten:** practise one capability in a bounded task.
4. **Clear / Wege freimachen:** address time, access, information, authority or incentive barriers.
5. **Gather / Am Lagerfeuer:** share chosen observations and decide what continues.

Use a weekly expedition with optional small activities as the first cadence hypothesis. Avoid mandatory daily tasks or completion pressure. Community peer groups can compare experiments between gatherings.

## The application method

**Choose a task → learn enough to try → rehearse → apply → get feedback → adapt → repeat in a changed situation → decide what becomes routine.**

Every mission needs:
- Recognisable work situation, role and intended result.
- One observable learning focus and explicit quality criteria.
- A small preparation example and a safe rehearsal.
- A bounded workplace attempt, including time and permitted tools/data.
- A feedback partner or review method.
- Evidence of what happened, including mistakes and inconclusive results.
- A second attempt with changed conditions and less support.
- A workplace request: what must others make possible?
- Stop condition, review date and route into ordinary work.

Diagnose before prescribing development: is the obstacle know-how, opportunity/resources, mandate/coordination, interests/incentives, or a mixture? A training recommendation is not the default answer to a structural blockage. Participants may act, seek an ally, escalate or stop. Agency is an option, not an obligation.

## Three starter tools

- **Skill Gap—or System Gap?** Start with one actual difficulty and distinguish learning needs from working conditions.
- **Permission to Practise:** agree a stretch task, protected time, feedback and boundaries.
- **Future Skill, Present Task:** translate a broad trend into a concrete task and a small experiment.

These tools support missions across domains; they are not validated diagnostic assessments.

## Personalisation

Adapt scenarios by role, actual task, experience, available support, tools, data rules and time. Use explicit choices and editable templates first. Do not infer a person's skills from activity or silently assign competence levels.

The same research-verification skill could concern a communications brief, an HR policy question or a research synthesis. Each needs different sources, review expertise and information boundaries.

## Wilderness design direction

Use field guides, trail markers, topographic lines, observation cards and campfire stories. Keep the feeling curious, adult and practical. Give all metaphors plain-language labels; a map must have an equivalent list. Exploration should never imply that individuals must endure hostile conditions.

The visual system should work in a browser, printed field card and ordinary organisational channels. No visual style or assets have been produced yet.

## What success would mean

Distinguish:
- Reach: people encountered or joined the campaign.
- Application: someone attempted a real task.
- Quality: the result met stated criteria after review.
- Learning/transfer: the person handled a changed case and explained their judgement.
- Conditions: the organisation removed a blocker or created a practice opportunity.
- Sustained use: an appropriate routine continued at a later review.

Attendance, completed cards, confidence and polished AI output alone do not demonstrate learning. Pilot results are local observations, not proof of general effectiveness.

## Ownership and privacy

A campaign sponsor owns responses to systemic barriers; facilitators support practice; participants choose what to share. Protected practice time belongs in campaign planning.

Keep private reflections separate from voluntary shared stories and aggregate campaign observations. No employee ranking, manager dashboard or automatic performance assessment. Do not collect confidential cases in this public repository. Local browser storage is not an access-control boundary on a shared device.

For AI missions, distinguish permitted technical capability from organisational approval. Start with synthetic material, move to real work only using approved tools and data, and keep humans responsible for consequential outputs.

## First pilot proposal

One consenting team, one AI scenario, two attempts and a later review. Compare task quality, review effort, explanation of decisions and ability to handle a changed case. Record blockers without naming people in public material. Stop if quality, consent, permitted data use or workload boundaries fail. No pilot is authorised or scheduled by this proposal.
