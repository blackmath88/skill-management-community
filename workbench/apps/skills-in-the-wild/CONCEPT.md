# Skills in the Wild

*Fähigkeiten entwickeln. Spielräume schaffen. Im Alltag erproben.*

Status: revised concept, 12 November 2026. The owner endorsed the umbrella, wilderness theme, campaign orientation and domain-specific scenarios. Positioning: professionally credible and engaging, with gamified mechanics but not presented as a game. Delivery details remain proposals. No campaign has been tested, app built or deployment authorised by this document.

## Purpose and positioning

Skills in the Wild is a campaign-based practice and capability-development programme for working life. People choose a meaningful task, use a small method, try it within clear boundaries, get feedback and return for a changed case. Teams address the conditions needed to make that practice possible and useful.

A campaign creates invitations and shared attention; repeated practice and organisational follow-through provide the substance. This may be a month such as Skillcember, a longer season or a series of team expeditions. Joining one useful activity must be possible without completing a programme. No December launch is committed.

Central invitation: Was in uns steckt, zeigt sich im Tun. Entdecke Fähigkeiten. Probiere Neues. Schaffe anderen den Spielraum dazu.

Describe the offer externally as a practice programme, development initiative or campaign. Do not call it a game, serious game or workplace RPG. Gamification is an internal design approach, not the product category or a claim of effectiveness. Professional standing comes from useful methods, transparent criteria, responsible boundaries and credible follow-through. Avoid childish rewards, theatrical role-play and inflated mastery claims.

## Two connected journeys

Every mission connects personal practice and shared conditions. Neither substitutes for the other.

| Journey | Central question | Visible development |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal practice | What can I now notice, do or judge differently? | Attempts, feedback, revised judgement and changed-case application |
| Shared conditions | What must change so this practice is possible and useful? | Access, protected time, authority, review support and shared routines |

For example, distinguishing decisions from proposals may require individual practice; establishing what a meeting actually decided may require a shared confirmation routine. Do not translate every difficulty into a personal skills gap.

## Entry and diagnosis

Offer three entry points: something in my work is harder than it should be; I want to try something new; our team wants to improve a shared practice. Ask only enough to suggest a useful next action.

Compare a successful and a difficult case where possible. Consider individual, team and organisational explanations. Ask whether a capable person could succeed with the same time, mandate, information and incentives.

| Starting situation | Suggested route |
| --- | --- |
| I do not know how to approach this | Worked example and rehearsal |
| I understand it but need practice | Bounded workplace attempt |
| I need another perspective | Peer observation or consultation |
| Conditions prevent action | Clear-the-path request |
| We disagree about good quality | Agree criteria before practising |
| The task may be inappropriate | Change approach or stop |

Routes are editable hypotheses, not validated diagnoses. Participants may act, seek an ally, request a decision, pause or stop. Agency is an option, not an obligation to overcome structural barriers.

## Campaign rhythm

1. Explore / Gelände erkunden: discover existing skills and useful expertise.
2. Spot / Spuren lesen: connect changing work to capability needs.
3. Try / Neues Terrain betreten: practise through a bounded task.
4. Clear / Wege freimachen: address time, access, authority and incentive barriers.
5. Gather / Am Lagerfeuer: share selected observations and decide what continues.

Start with a weekly expedition and optional small activities as a cadence hypothesis. Avoid mandatory daily tasks, streaks and completion pressure. Peer groups can compare experiments between gatherings.

## The application method

Choose a task → learn enough to try → rehearse → apply → get feedback → adapt → repeat in a changed situation → decide what becomes routine.

Every mission needs a recognisable situation and intended result; one observable learning focus; explicit quality criteria; a small example and safe rehearsal; a bounded attempt with permitted tools, data and time; feedback; evidence including mistakes and uncertainty; a changed-case attempt with appropriately reduced guidance; a check on enabling conditions; and a stopping condition, review date and route into ordinary work.

Embed four questions adapted from Guerrilla: who benefits and who needs a say; what would count as improvement; when should we stop or revise; and what happens if this is worth keeping?

Generating a field card is preparation, not mission completion. The app must make returning after an attempt as clear as starting one.

## Engagement and progression

Use exploration, meaningful choice, visible progress, useful methods, peer contribution and milestones. These are proposed motivational design conventions, not validated measures of competence.

An expedition log records situations explored, attempts, feedback, revised judgements, changed cases and support requested or provided. It preserves context and counterevidence rather than assigning a stable personal level.

A field kit contains reusable methods: a source-checking guide, briefing canvas, decision/proposal checklist, peer-review protocol and permission-to-practise agreement. Each includes an example and can link to an occasion on which it was used. Access to necessary methods must not depend on earning rewards.

Milestones may mark a first attempt, useful correction, changed-case review, support request or adopted routine. Use restrained language and optional recognition. Corrections and justified stopping decisions can be meaningful progress. No XP-based competence claims, competitive leaderboards, compulsory sharing, streak penalties or automatic skill inference.

## Organisational follow-through

A clear-the-path card contains the task, blocking condition, smallest useful change requested, responsible decision role, review date and response: agreed, alternative offered, declined or unresolved. A printable card is sufficient for the first static build; a shared tracking service is not required.

The sponsor owns arranging responses to systemic barriers, not access to private learning records. Facilitators support practice and route requests. Participants choose what to share. Protected time belongs in campaign planning. A blocked attempt with a clear request is legitimate, not personal failure. Requests must not expose participants to unnecessary risk or require escalation.

## Three starter tools

- Skill Gap—or System Gap? Distinguish learning needs from working conditions.
- Permission to Practise: agree a stretch task, protected time, feedback and boundaries.
- Future Skill, Present Task: translate a trend into a concrete task and small experiment.

These support missions across domains; they are not validated assessments.

## AI edition and judgement

Keep the six scenarios in the linked AI edition. Connect them through task framing, appropriate delegation, verification, human responsibility and workflow design. Prompt production is not the learning objective.

For the starter mission, propose a short initial classification before the worked answer; review of a plausible but flawed AI-style draft; explanation of corrections; a changed case with less guidance; and a decision about appropriate workplace use. Ask which uncertainties require another person's clarification rather than another prompt. Record assistance used: example, template, peer or AI. A small unaided judgement task may inform reflection but does not imply that professional expertise requires tool-free work. Detailed scenario revisions belong in the AI edition before implementation.

## Practice history and state

Separate mission templates, personal mission instances, attempts, observations, condition requests and review decisions. Preserve earlier attempts when adding another; do not overwrite history with the latest interpretation.

Keep separate dimensions:
- Activity: planned, attempted, reviewed or paused.
- Observed result: criteria met, mixed, not met or unknown.
- Conditions: available, constrained, blocked or not checked.

Unknown, unobserved, unsuccessful and blocked are different states. Record context, date, assistance and sharing status alongside observations. A changed interpretation may represent development without a new level.

## Personalisation and visual direction

Adapt by role, task, experience, support, approved tools, data rules and time through explicit choices and editable templates. Do not infer competence from activity. Different roles require different sources, reviewers and information boundaries.

Use field guides, trail markers, topographic lines, observation cards and optional campfire stories. Keep the presentation adult, precise, curious and professionally credible. Provide plain-language labels, an equivalent list for any map, keyboard access and readable mobile and print layouts. Exploration must not imply enduring hostile conditions. The visual system should work in the browser and ordinary organisational channels. No assets or finished visual style are asserted by this concept.

## First-build priority

Retain the separate static app and selective-port direction in the build handoff. Prioritise campaign entry, one synthetic mission workspace, a return view, a two-attempt expedition log and a clear-the-path card. Other scenarios remain previews. Invest in returning and reviewing before a rich campaign map. No backend, accounts, live AI integration, analytics or deployment in the baseline.

Guerrilla supplies useful content-driven fields, navigation, print patterns and intervention boundaries. Its meeting-specific derivations, fixed storage identity and linear card endpoint are not the mission model. Adapt rather than copy wholesale; verify reuse permission, provenance and behaviour.

Additional acceptance criteria: return without reconstructing the case; preserve attempt one after attempt two; show assistance beside observations; record inability to attempt without reporting failure; allow a justified stop to AI use; export requests without private reflections; distinguish better output from changed judgement. These test product behaviour, not intervention effectiveness. The linked handoff remains the technical brief and should be reconciled with this revision before execution.

## Success, ownership and privacy

Distinguish reach, attempted application, reviewed quality, learning/transfer into a changed case, improved conditions and sustained appropriate use. Attendance, confidence, completed cards and polished AI output alone do not demonstrate learning. Local pilots do not prove general effectiveness.

Keep private reflections separate from voluntary shared stories and campaign observations. No employee ranking, manager performance dashboard or automatic assessment. Do not collect confidential cases in this public repository. Local browser storage is not an access-control boundary on shared devices. Export only explicitly selected material.

For AI, distinguish technical possibility from organisational approval. Start with synthetic material; use real work only with approved tools, suitable data and responsible human review. No private reflection is silently included in copied prompts.

## First pilot proposal

One consenting team, one AI scenario, two attempts and a later review. Compare quality, total effort including review, explanations and changed-case judgement; record assistance and blockers. Check whether support requests receive a response. Stop if quality, consent, permitted data use or workload boundaries fail. No pilot is authorised or scheduled.
