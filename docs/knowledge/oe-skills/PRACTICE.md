# From research and community exchange to practice

All exercises below are proposals informed by R1 §§5–11 and R2 §§5–8. They have not been run or scheduled. University examples are hypothetical, not reports of existing institutional problems.

## A diagnosis before a programme

1. Name a concrete work situation and the result that should improve.
2. Compare a successful and a difficult case.
3. Form at least one individual, team and organisational hypothesis.
4. Ask whether a capable person could succeed with the same time, mandate, information and incentives.
5. Identify who can change the relevant conditions and who must participate in the decision.
6. Choose one reversible intervention and a review signal.
7. Check observed behaviour, result quality, effort and unintended effects separately.

## Three experiments to consider

| Experiment | Small scope | Expected evidence | Stop / revise / retain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Taxonomy usefulness | One decision; six synthetic cases; two practitioners | Which distinctions change decisions, where classification differs, effort required | Remove unused distinctions; revise ambiguous ones. Discard temporary person mappings |
| Peer learning with follow-through | Three consenting peers; one de-identified case each; two meetings | A tested action, observed result, revised hypothesis and organisational obstacle | Stop if useful cases cannot be shared safely; revise if sessions produce talk without feasible action |
| Capability or conditions? | One recurring handover between two willing teams | Compare cases before and after clarifying one decision right or information requirement | Stop if burden exceeds value; retain only if another person can use the routine without the original champion |

Agree participation, information boundaries and local authority before any trial. These small exercises test feasibility and mechanisms, not causal effectiveness at organisational scale.

## Methods before tooling

| Need | Start with | Add software when | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understand failure paths | Critical-incident conversation and process sketch | Many cases require structured comparison | Recollection is not observation |
| Locate relevant skills | Small matrix with behaviour, evidence date and context | Repeated decisions justify maintenance | Self-ratings do not prove competence |
| Explore shared meaning | Capability map with local examples | Versioning / relationships become hard to manage manually | Neat structure is not validity |
| Improve judgement | Peer consultation and after-action review | Coordination becomes a demonstrated bottleneck | Attendance is not transfer |
| Maintain knowledge | Curated repository with source and claim status | Search and permissions exceed simple documentation | Retrieval is not endorsement |
| Test learning around AI | Comparable synthetic tasks with and without assistance | A repeated evaluation process is justified | Assisted performance does not establish independent expertise |

## Candidate OE practice map

This is a draft for discussion, not an assessment or a change to Leader Me's application skills. Compare it with the OD Network framework identified in R2 before adopting it.

| Capability | Observable practice | Evidence limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract the work | Clarifies purpose, authority, affected groups and boundaries | A clear agreement may still be unjust or ineffective |
| Investigate the system | Tests structural explanations alongside individual ones | A few interviews do not prove causality |
| Enable dialogue | Makes conflicting interpretations discussable and consequential | Speaking in one meeting does not establish safety |
| Design experiments | States hypothesis, action, signal, review and stopping condition | A small trial does not prove general effectiveness |
| Enable application | Addresses time, access, authority and reinforcement | Opportunity alone does not establish skill |
| Evaluate and adapt | Preserves counterevidence and revises the next action | A reflective account is not an objective personality assessment |

## Record a lesson without turning it into a score

Use: situation → observation → interpretation → competing explanation → next action → result → revised interpretation. Keep source, context, assistance used, date and sharing status alongside the record.

For Leader Me, retain the difference between historical attempts and current capability hypotheses. A changed interpretation can represent development without adding a level. Unknown, unobserved, unsuccessful and blocked by conditions are different states.

## Questions for the community

1. Which organisational decision improved after you introduced skills language?
2. What did you deliberately leave out of your taxonomy?
3. When did a supposed skills gap turn out to be a mandate or incentive problem?
4. What did the sponsor actually change?
5. How do you distinguish learning from tool-assisted performance?
6. What evidence changed your original intervention design?
7. How do peer groups connect learning to real action without exposing private cases?
8. What remains usable when the programme, consultant or original champion leaves?
