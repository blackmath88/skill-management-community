# Future-skills trend radar and organisational capability needs

Status: participant idea recorded on 11 November 2026; speaker not identified. Not an agreed programme or a verified method.

## Idea from the community

Create a trend radar for future skills, then mirror it against the organisation's own capabilities to form a picture of what it wants or needs to develop. This is a paraphrase of the owner's live note, not a quotation.

## Working interpretation

The useful bridge is from an external signal to a concrete change in work, then to a capability requirement. A popular trend alone does not establish an organisational need. Compare desired capabilities with current evidence and the conditions for using them; do not assume that an absent profile entry proves a skill gap.

| Stage | Question | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| External signal | What is changing, on what evidence and over what horizon? | Source, date, uncertainty and contrary signals |
| Local relevance | Which strategic outcome, task or role could change? | Concrete scenario and affected work |
| Capability hypothesis | What must people or teams be able to do under those conditions? | Observable behaviour and necessary organisational conditions |
| Current picture | What can we already demonstrate, and what remains unknown? | Work examples, context and evidence limits |
| Target picture | Which capabilities do we want to sustain, develop, access externally or stop prioritising? | Rationale, decision owner and review horizon |
| Response | Is the useful intervention learning, work redesign, recruitment, partnership or observation? | Small next step and review signal |

## Connection to taxonomy and leadership

Use the radar to test whether taxonomy categories help an actual decision. Maintain a small common vocabulary while allowing local examples. Leadership sponsorship becomes concrete when leaders select relevant scenarios, make trade-offs and enable time and authority to respond. See [taxonomy and leadership](TAXONOMY-AND-LEADERSHIP.md).

## Small experiment to consider

Choose one organisational priority, three sourced trend signals and one willing team. Map each signal to a changed task, a capability hypothesis and existing evidence. Review which signals actually change a decision. Retain the reasoning and uncertainty, not a ranking of employees. This is proposed, not scheduled or authorised.

Questions for the next discussion:

- Who maintains the radar and challenges fashionable but weak signals?
- What horizon matters for our decisions?
- How do local teams contest relevance or describe capabilities in their own terms?
- What evidence would make us revise or retire a target capability?

Boundary: the external radar can be shareable while the internal capability picture contains sensitive strategy or personnel information. Keep company-specific mappings outside this public repository unless explicitly cleared for publication.
