# Taxonomy granularity and leadership sponsorship

Origin: the owner's notes from the PostFinance input on 11 November 2026. The notes identify taxonomy as a central challenge, often too broad or too specific, and leadership / C-level buy-in as a lever. These are participant paraphrases, not verified quotations or a complete account of PostFinance's programme. Speaker identity and examples remain unknown. See [community record](COMMUNITY.md).

## Taxonomy: specific enough for which decision?

Our interpretation: a taxonomy's usefulness depends on the decisions it supports. Precision that helps task assignment may burden a strategic conversation. A shared word can hide different expectations; a highly detailed catalogue can become expensive to maintain.

| Use | Useful unit of description | Minimum useful evidence | Common mismatch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Development conversation | Behaviour in a concrete situation | Example, context and next opportunity | Abstract label such as “collaboration” |
| Project staffing | Task, scope, responsibility and available capacity | Relevant work plus willingness / availability | Skill match treated as permission to allocate someone |
| Recruitment | Job-relevant demand and assessment criteria | Appropriate work sample | Inference from credentials or keywords alone |
| Strategic capability planning | Organisational result and dependencies | Scenario, process constraints and coverage | Thousands of micro-skills mistaken for strategy |
| Peer learning | A practice question peers can explore | A shareable case and attempted action | Standardising everyone's private development profile |

This table is a design heuristic informed by R1 §§1, 4, 7, not a validated universal architecture.

## Proposed layered model

1. A small common core for cross-organisational navigation.
2. Local capability descriptions owned by professional communities or teams.
3. Concrete task and behaviour examples where decisions require detail.

Keep stable IDs, synonyms, parent relationships and version history. Allow local terms to remain distinct until equivalence is demonstrated. Record why an entry was split, merged or retired; do not silently change the meaning of historical evidence.

These are proposed information-management rules, not claims about PostFinance's implementation.

## Smallest useful taxonomy experiment

Proposed, not scheduled or authorised: choose one recurring decision and six de-identified or synthetic cases. Ask two practitioners to use a broad and a more specific description to make the decision. Record ambiguity, time spent, disagreements, missed distinctions and maintenance effort.

Expected useful result: identify which distinctions actually change the decision. Merge categories nobody uses differently; split only where a meaningful decision is otherwise lost. A small pilot tests usefulness, not psychometric validity. Discard temporary person mappings afterwards; retain only the agreed terms and rationale.

## Leadership: turn endorsement into observable commitments

| Sponsorship dimension | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Priority | A consequential work problem and explicit trade-offs are named |
| Time and capacity | People can practise during work; other work is reduced or reprioritised |
| Authority | Someone can change conflicting rules, access or decision rights |
| Resource mobility | Teams can release expertise rather than hoard it |
| Role modelling | Leaders seek feedback, expose uncertainty and revise their own approach |
| Follow-through | Reviews examine application and obstacles as well as participation |
| Trust | Development data has an agreed purpose and contestable interpretations |

Hypothesis: senior sponsorship helps when it changes these conditions. It is neither proof of implementation nor sufficient by itself; middle managers and professional peers affect daily practice. Compare this hypothesis with R1 §§4–6 and Implement's published systemic leadership position [V3](EVIDENCE.md).

- What is intentionally left outside the taxonomy?

The permission boundary matters: making expertise discoverable does not automatically authorise staffing, evaluation or access to the underlying development notes.
